ABSTRACT  
 
Global supply chains are undergoing rapid digital transformation under the paradigm of 
Logistics 4.0. Despite this progress, Small and Medium -sized Enterprises (SMEs) 
continue to face significant challenges in accessing and participating in international trade. 
Regulatory complexity, extensive documentation requirements, limited access to trade 
finance, and fragmented digital systems increase operational friction and reduce 
competitiveness  for SMEs  in global  markets.  
 
This project presents TradeOS , an agentic, AI -driven B2B marketplace designed to 
automate, streamline, and secure the complete trade lifecycle for SMEs. The proposed 
system is structured as a tri-layered architecture consisting of: (1) a Presentation Layer 
enabling verified partner discovery, (2) an Intelligence Layer incorporating predictive 
analytics and forecasting models, and (3) an Automation Layer that manages end -to-end 
trade execution.  
 
 
At the core of TradeOS is an Agentic AI Co -pilot , powered by a Multi -Agent System 
(MAS) capable of coordinating trade processes autonomously. The system integrates 
specialized agents such as the DocuAgent , which employs Transformer -based NLP for 
automated documentation analysis and HS Code classification; the RiskAgent , which 
performs multi -factor, real -time risk assessment using stochastic modelling; the 
LogisticsAgent , which computes optimal logistics routes; and the EscrowAgent , which 
ensures secure, milestone -based payment settlement through smart contracts on a 
Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT) Trust Layer . 
 
The project follows the Agile Scrum methodology , with iterative development and 
continuous validation. A pilot test route  (Bengaluru → Hamburg)  is used  to evaluate  system 
performance.  
 
Overall, TradeOS aims to provide SMEs with a unified, intelligent, and secure platform for 
global  trade, enhancing  supply  chain resilience and setting  a new benchmark for transparent 
and user - centric trade automation.

1  Chapter  1: Introduction  
 
1.1 Introduction  
Small and Medium -sized Enterprises (SMEs) are critical drivers of global trade, yet they continue to 
face barriers such as regulatory complexity, lack of reliable partners, financial risks, and limited 
supply chain visibility. These challenges restrict their ability to compete with larger corporations in 
international markets. Recent advancements in Artificial Intelligence, blockchain and multi -agent 
systems offer an opportunity to simplify and automate the trade process. However, most digital trade 
platforms are designed for large enterprises, leaving SMEs underserved.  
 
This project, TradeOS , proposes an agentic B2B marketplace that integrates a cognitive co -pilot 
powered by multi -agent systems to manage the entire trade lifecycle —from compliance and 
documentation to logistics and payment settlement. The aim is to democratize global trade by 
reducing operational friction and enabling SMEs to participate more efficiently in international 
markets  
 
 
1.2 Problem  Statement  
The participation of Small and Medium -sized Enterprises (SMEs) in the global value chain is 
critically restricted by information asymmetry and operational complexities . Despite their 
significant contribution  to the economy,  SMEs  face challenges  that prevent  them  from  fully  engaging 
in international trade. The major issues addressed in this research are as follows:  
 
• SMEs  struggle  to interpret  and comply  with diverse  international  trade  regulations,  tariffs,  and 
customs documentation standards, leading to costly delays and penalties. 
• SMEs are highly vulnerable to financial risks such as currency fluctuations and lack access to 
advanced trade finance instruments. 
• Lack  of end-to-end supply  chain  visibility  exposes SMEs  to disruptions  caused  by geopolitical 
instability, extreme weather, and logistical bottlenecks.  
 
The core problem is the absence of an intelligent, automated, and collaborative platform that 
integrates partner discovery, regulatory compliance, risk assessment, and financial settlement into a 
unified workflow, specifically for SMEs

The main  objectives of  the project are: 
 
• To design a tri-layered system architecture (Marketplace, Intelligence, Automation) for a

• Agent  Development:  Implementing  core logic  for five key agents:  MatchmakerAgent,  
DocuAgent, RiskAgent, LogisticsAgent, and EscrowAgent.  
• Validation:  Demonstrating  the complete  trade  process  through  a sample  trade  scenario.  
• Technology  Stack:  Using  Python,  FastAPI,  and agent -frameworks  such as LangChain  or

3  Chapter  2: Literature  Survey  
 
2.1 Existing  Work  and Research  Gaps  
Existing literature highlights significant advancements in individual components of global trade 
automation, such as AI -based HS code classification, blockchain -enabled traceability, and multi - 
agent systems for supply chain decision -making. However, these solutions remain fragmented and  
are not designed specifically for SMEs.  
 
Key gaps identified  include:  
 
• Lack  of integration  across  compliance,  logistics,  risk modelling,  and payments.  
• Limited  automation,  resulting  in continued  dependency  on manual workflows.  
• High  complexity  and cost of existing  ERP/SCM  platforms,  making  them  unsuitable  for 
SMEs.  
• Absence of an end -to-end intelligent system that can autonomously execute complete trade  
workflows.  
 
2.2 Analysis  of Existing  Systems  
Current  B2B trade  systems  suffer  from  operational  fragmentation  and limited  intelligence:  
 
a) Manual  Brokerage  Processes  
 
SMEs commonly rely on emails, spreadsheets, and paper -based coordination for freight forwarding, 
customs clearance, and payments. These disconnected practices lead to frequent errors, poor 
visibility, and heavy reliance on intermediaries.  
 
b) Digital  Marketplaces  
 
Platforms like Alibaba.com provide global supplier discovery but lack automated compliance 
verification, logistics planning, and risk assessment. Execution remains largely manual.  
 
c) ERP  and SCM  Platforms  
 
Enterprise -grade solutions such as SAP Ariba and Oracle SCM offer integrated workflows but are 
costly, resource -intensive, and too complex for SME adoption.  

--- PAGE 11 ---
4  d) Specialized  Digital  Tools  
Standalone tools exist for HS code classification (NLP -based), blockchain trade finance, and multi - 
agent logistics research. However, they operate independently and lack interoperability, forcing 
SMEs to manually manage multiple systems.  
 
Summary:  
No existing system provides SMEs with a unified, intelligent, and fully automated trade execution  
environment. This highlights the need for an integrated agentic platform like TradeOS.  
 
 
2.3 Limitation s of Existing System  
 
• Lack of Integration Across Functions  
Current systems operate in silos, where key processes such as customs Lack documentation, 
logistics management, and payment handling are not integrated into a unified platform. This 
fragmentation forces SMEs to rely on multiple tools and platforms, resulting in inefficiencies, 
increased operational complexity, and higher chances of data inconsistency.  
 
• Not Tailored for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs)  
Most existing solutions are designed to meet the needs of large enterprises, making them expensive 
and technically complex for SMEs. Small businesses often lack the financial and technical resources 
to adopt such systems, limiting their participation in global trade.  
 
• Limited Automation and High Manual Dependency  
Although partial automation exists, many critical processes still require manual intervention. Tasks 
such as customs documentation, compliance verification, and transaction processing are often 
performed manually, leading to delays, human errors, and reduced productivity.  
 
• Lack of Trust and Transparency  
Existing systems do not provide a comprehensive mechanism for verifying the authenticity and 
credibility of trading partners. This lack of transparency increases the risk of fraud and disputes, 
especially in cross -border transactions involving unfamiliar entities.

6  Chapter  3: Proposed  System  
 
3.1 Overview  of the Proposed  System  
TradeOS is a Smart Agentic B2B Marketplace designed to enable Small and Medium -sized 
Enterprises (SMEs) to participate efficiently and confidently in global trade. The platform directly 
connects verified international buyers and sellers, removing intermediaries and improving 
transparency, trust, and operational control.The system integrates an Agentic AI Engine that 
automates complex trade tasks such as documentation, HS Code classification, compliance checks, 
logistics planning, risk evaluation, and secure payment execution. By replacing manual workflows 
with autonomous software agents, TradeOS establishes a reliable, efficient, and intelligence -driven 
trade ecosystem that enhances SME competitiveness.  
 
Technically, TradeOS consolidates fragmented trade operations into a coordinated multi -agent 
execution framework . Each phase of the trade lifecycle —partner discovery, document generation, 
risk modelling, logistics routing, and settlement —is handled by agents that collaborate and make 
context -aware decisions in real time.  
 
3.2 Key Features  
The TradeOS platform incorporates several advanced features to enable seamless, secure, and 
intelligent trade execution for SMEs:  
• Smart B2B Marketplace  : A digital platform that connects verified buyers and sellers 
globally, eliminating intermediaries and improving trust and transparency.  
• Agentic AI Automation  : The system employs multiple intelligent agents to automate 
critical trade processes such as partner discovery, documentation, risk analysis, logistics 
planning, and payment handling.  
• HS Code Classification and Document Generation  : Automated classification of goods 
using AI models and generation of customs -compliant documents, reducing manual effort 
and errors.  
• Risk Analysis and Predictive Modelling  : Real-time evaluation of risks based on external 
data sources such as weather conditions, port congestion, and market fluctuations.

7  • Optimized Logistics Planning  : Intelligent route selection and shipment planning using 
APIs and optimization algorithms to minimize cost and delivery time.  
• End-to-End Integration  : All stages of the trade lifecycle are integrated into a single 
platform, improving efficiency and reducing dependency on multiple systems.  
 
3.3 Proposed System Workflow  
The operational  workflow  of TradeOS  is executed  through  a coordinated  set of intelligent  agents. 
These agents communicate asynchronously, enabling scalable automation and fault tolerance.  
 
Workflow  Steps:  
Step  1: SME  Onboarding  
 
The platform performs digital KYC and verification of trade credentials using rule -based logic and  
API-supported authentication, ensuring that only legitimate businesses access the marketplace.  
 
Step  2: Trade  Partner Discovery  
 
The MatchmakerAgent identifies optimal partners using similarity scoring, product taxonomy 
filters, and historical interaction heuristics. This reduces information asymmetry and accelerates 
partner selection.  
 
Step  3: Deal  Initiation  
 
Once  trade  terms  are agreed  upon,  the Agentic  Co-pilot  activates  and begins  orchestrating  all 
downstream operations.  
 
Step  4: Autonomous  Multi -Agent  Execution  
A coordinated  set of intelligent  agents  handles  mission -critical  tasks:  
 
• DocuAgent : Performs  HS Code  classification  using  transformer -based  NLP  and generates 
structured customs documents.  
• RiskAgent : Conducts  risk estimation  using  real-time data (weather,  port congestion, 
currency volatility).  
• LogisticsAgent : Computes  optimal  shipping  routes  by interfacing  with logistics  APIs  and 
applying risk constraint s. 
• EscrowAgent : Executes  secure,  milestone -based payments  via blockchain smart  contracts.  
 
Agents  communicate  asynchronously  to ensure  modular,  scalable,  and fault-tolerant execution.  
 

--- PAGE 15 ---
8  Step  5: Completion  and Settlement  
Upon delivery confirmation, the EscrowAgent verifies the final state and releases payment 
automatically  through smart  contract  logic. This  ensures  immutability,  transparency, and  dispute -free 
settlement.  
 
3.4 Trade  Lifecycle  Automation  Framework  
TradeOS automates every  phase  of cross -border  trade:  
 
1. Verification  Layer  – Validates  SMEs  before  entry.  
2. Discovery  Layer  – Identifies  compatible  partners algorithmically.  
3. Orchestration  Layer  – Activates  the Agentic  Co-pilot to execute  tasks.  
4. Execution  Layer  – Agents  perform  documentation,  risk analysis,  routing,  and payment 
control.  
5. Settlement  Layer  – Smart  contracts  finalize  payments  securely.  
 
This framework  ensures  deterministic  automation,  reducing  errors  and manual  bottlenecks.

14  Chapter  5: System Design  
 
 
5.1 System  Architecture  
TradeOS is designed using a three -layer distributed architecture supported by an Agentic 
Intelligence Layer and a Multi -Agent Automation Engine . This separation of concerns ensures 
efficient processing, secure data handling, and robust multi -agent coordination.  
 
Tri-Layer  Architecture  
 
 
1. Presentation  Layer  (User  Interaction  Interface)  
 
• Built  using  React.js and API-driven  communication.  
• Handles  SME  onboarding,  KYC  verification,  partner  discovery,  trade  initiation,  and tracking 
dashboards.  
• Communication  occurs  through  an API gateway  for secure  access  control.  
 
2. Intelligence  Layer  (Cognitive  Decision  Core)  
 
• Hosts  AI-driven  components:  
o Transformer -based  HS Code  classification  
o NLP -driven  document  parsing  
o Stochastic  risk prediction  models  
o Market  and logistics  analytics  
• Provides  inference  outputs to  the Automation  Layer.  
• Enables  reasoning, context  sharing,  and dynamic decision -making.  
 
3. Automation  Layer  (Agentic  Co-Pilot)  
 
• Implements autonomous  trade  operations  using  a Multi -Agent  System  (MAS)  built with 
LangChain/Autogen.  
• Each  agent  executes  domain -specific  tasks  and communicates  through  an event -driven 
messaging bus (Kafka/RabbitMQ).  
• Ensures  deterministic,  rule-driven  execution of  trade  workflows.

agent APIs.  
5. Data  & Integration  Layer  : 
 
Event bus / message queue (Kafka or RabbitMQ) for asynchronous workflows, ETL pipelines, 
and microservices.  Connectors  for external  systems:  customs  databases,  carrier  APIs,  market  data 
feeds, and payment gateways.  
6. Trust  & Persistence  Layer : 
 
Transactional  database  (Postgres)  for core records,  time-series  store  for telemetry,  object  store  for 
documents, and a distributed ledger / blockchain module for tamper -proof audit trails and escrow 
smart contracts.

27  Chapter  8: Result And Analysis  
To empirically validate the effectiveness of the TradeOS multi -agent automation framework, a 
structured, evidence -based evaluation methodology was followed. The goal of this validation is to 
demonstrate that the coordinated agentic workflow of TradeOS provides measurable improvements 
over conventional manual or rule -based trade processes. All tests were executed under controlled, 
repeatable conditions to ensure scientific reliability.  
 
8.1 Baseline  for Comparison:  The “Manual  Workflow  Model”  
 
To establish  a meaningful  benchmark,  a baseline Manual  Workflow  Model  was defined.  This 
model represents the traditional SME trade process, characterised by:  
 
8.1.1 Manual  partner  identification  
8.1.2 Manual  document  checking  and HS classification  
8.1.3 No predictive  risk modelling  
8.1.4 Static  logistics routing  based solely  on least cost 
8.1.5 Manual  bank  transfers  without  contract -backed  safeguards  
 
This baseline captures the existing industry challenges and serves as the control model against  
which TradeOS is evaluated. Comparing TradeOS to this baseline enables quantifiable proof of 
automation, speed, and accuracy improvements introduced by the agentic architecture.  
 
8.2 Trade  Simulation  Environment  
A controlled  simulation  environment  was created  to enable  repeatable  and realistic  validation  of the 
system.  
A. Trade Route Scenario : A standardized Bengaluru → Hamburg route was used, incorporating 
multiple   shipping   paths,   weather   variability,   port  delays,   and  customs   requirements.  
B. Document Dataset : Synthetic, industry -formatted invoices and packing lists were prepared to 
test   OCR    accuracy,    HS   Code    classification,    and   document    generation.  
C. Logistics & Risk Data Simulator : A Python simulator produced dynamic inputs such as 
weather changes, port congestion levels, and currency volatility, enabling controlled testing of the 
RiskAgent and LogisticsAgent.

29  Chapter  8: Conclusion  
 
The proposed TradeOS platform demonstrates the effective integration of multiple autonomous AI agents 
to streamline and optimize the end -to-end international trade process. By leveraging structured trade 
datasets such as UN Comtrade  along with real -time data sources like OpenWeather  and ExchangeRate -
API, the system transitions from static decision -making to a dynamic, data -driven framework.  
 
The architecture combines specialized agents —Matchmaker, Negotiation, Risk Assessment, Document 
Generation, and Logistics —each responsible for a critical stage in the trade lifecycle. This modular 
approach ensures scalability, flexibility, and robustness while maintaining transparency in decision -
making. The Risk Agent enhances reliability by incorporating multi -dimensional risk factors, while the 
Logistics Agent optimizes transportation planning through intelligent route and mode selection using 
platforms such as OpenStreetMap . 
 
Furthermore, the system successfully reduces manual intervention by automating complex processes such 
as supplier selection, price negotiation, compliance documentation, and shipment planning. Although 
certain elements rely on synthetic or semi -realistic datasets due to limitations in accessing real -world 
proprietary trade data, the overall framework closely simulates real -world trade operations and provides a 
strong foundation for future deployment.  
 
In conclusion, TradeOS represents a significant step toward intelligent, autonomous trade ecosystems, 
offering improved efficiency, reduced risk, and enhanced decision support. Future enhancements may 
include deeper integration of real -time APIs, machine learning –based predictive models, and direct 
connectivity with financial and logistics service providers to further increase system accuracy and real -
world applicability.
